# Ouroboros and Noroboro: exact solutions and a normalized cycle

**Matthew Chenoweth Wright**  
Original article: February 5, 2025  
Mathematical revision: October 13, 2026  
Revision prepared with AI assistance at the author's request.

## Abstract

The original Ouroboros proposal combines a phase reversal, a Fourier series, and a recurrence intended to represent cyclic persistence. We specify the domain and solve that recurrence exactly. At integer steps the Fourier factor equals the Riemann zeta function, so the proposed closed cycle instead has increasing magnitude. A stated normalization produces a genuine two-cycle. The related Noroboro recurrence has an exponent quadratic in the step index because its time-dependent growth multiplier compounds. These results establish properties of explicit mathematical sequences. They do not establish a new topology, fractal geometry, physical law, or metaphysical necessity.

## 1. Frozen target and assumptions

The target is the final pair of equations in [the February 5, 2025 article](https://enuminous.github.io/medium/2025-02-05_Derivation-of-the-Ouroboros-Equation-ec3bc131be46.html), preserved in `archive/2025-02-05-original.html` with provenance in `PROVENANCE.md`:

$$O(t)=e^{i\pi}O(t-1)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i2\pi nt}}{n^x},$$

$$N(t)=e^{\alpha t}e^{i\pi}N(t-1)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i2\pi nt}}{n^x}.$$

The article does not fix the time domain or the domain of $x$. This revision explicitly studies integer time steps $t=k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$, with updates for $k\geq1$. We rename $x$ to $s$ to distinguish a series exponent from a spatial coordinate. We assume fixed real $s>1$, complex initial data, and fixed real $\alpha\geq0$. Statements about expansion use $\alpha>0$ and nonzero initial data. A zero initial value remains zero.

The step index and exponent parameters are dimensionless. A physical time interpretation would require a time scale and a specified observable. The article supplies neither; this revision does not infer them.

## 2. Identify and control the series

Define, for real $t$,

$$F_s(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi int}}{n^s}.$$

For real $s>1$, the series converges absolutely and uniformly in real $t$ because each term has magnitude $n^{-s}$ and the comparison series converges. The defining polylogarithm series gives

$$F_s(t)=\operatorname{Li}_s(e^{2\pi it}).$$

This is an established special function, not a newly derived series [1]. It is one-periodic. At every integer $k$,

$$e^{2\pi ink}=1,\qquad F_s(k)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}=\zeta(s).$$

For finite real $s>1$, $\zeta(s)>1$: its first term is one and all remaining terms are positive [2]. For real $s\leq1$, the series at integer times diverges. Analytic continuation would define a different prescription and cannot silently replace the divergent series.

## 3. Exact solution of the original Ouroboros recurrence

Using $e^{i\pi}=-1$ gives

$$O_k=-\zeta(s)O_{k-1}.$$

**Proposition 1.** For all integers $k\geq0$,

$$O_k=(-1)^k\zeta(s)^kO_0.$$

**Proof.** The formula holds at $k=0$. Multiplication by $-\zeta(s)$ takes the expression for step $k-1$ to the expression for step $k$. Induction completes the proof.

For $O_0\neq0$, the magnitude is $\zeta(s)^k|O_0|$ and therefore grows exponentially with the step index. The phase reverses at every update. In particular,

$$O_{k+2}=\zeta(s)^2O_k\neq O_k.$$

Thus the final equation does not implement a nonzero closed two-cycle under this interpretation. A recurring sign pattern is insufficient to establish recurrence of the full state.

## 4. Explicit normalization and the cycle theorem

Define a modified multiplier

$$\widehat F_s(t)=\frac{F_s(t)}{\zeta(s)}$$

and a modified recurrence

$$\widehat O_k=-\widehat F_s(k)\widehat O_{k-1}.$$

This division is a revision to the original model, not an algebraic identity applied to it. Since $\widehat F_s(k)=1$ at integer steps,

$$\widehat O_k=-\widehat O_{k-1}.$$

**Proposition 2.** The normalized sequence satisfies

$$\widehat O_k=(-1)^k\widehat O_0,\qquad
|\widehat O_k|=|\widehat O_0|,\qquad
\widehat O_{k+2}=\widehat O_k.$$

**Proof.** Repeated multiplication by $-1$ yields the first identity. Taking absolute values yields the second. Multiplying twice by $-1$ yields the third. Nonzero initial data have minimal period two; zero initial data give a fixed point.

The normalization removes all dependence on $s$ from the integer-step dynamics. Consequently, these discrete observations cannot identify $s$. The surviving dynamics are the standard two-cycle generated by the map $z\mapsto-z$ on $\mathbb C$.

### What changes away from integer times?

The triangle inequality gives $|\widehat F_s(t)|\leq1$. For noninteger real $t$, the inequality is strict: the first and second summands have distinct phases, so their weighted sum already has magnitude strictly below the sum of their magnitudes. Adding the remaining terms cannot restore equality.

Therefore normalization by $\zeta(s)$ does not give constant magnitude at arbitrary real times. If the equation is interpreted as a delay relation, each fractional-time class $r+k$ has its own multiplier $-\widehat F_s(r)$ because $F_s$ is one-periodic. Noninteger classes decay in magnitude under forward unit steps. A real-time model also requires initial history, not just a single initial value. The cycle theorem is explicitly an integer-step result.

## 5. Exact solution of the Noroboro recurrence

At integer steps, the original equation becomes

$$N_k=-\zeta(s)e^{\alpha k}N_{k-1}.$$

**Proposition 3.** Its exact solution is

$$N_k=(-1)^k\zeta(s)^k
\exp\left(\frac{\alpha k(k+1)}2\right)N_0.$$

**Proof.** Multiply the step factors from $j=1$ to $j=k$. There are $k$ factors of $-\zeta(s)$, while the product of exponentials is $\exp(\alpha\sum_{j=1}^k j)$. Use $\sum_{j=1}^k j=k(k+1)/2$.

For nonzero initial data and $\alpha>0$, the log magnitude grows quadratically in $k$. Growth is therefore faster than $e^{ck}$ for every fixed real $c$. Replacing the Fourier factor by its normalized form removes only the factor $\zeta(s)^k$:

$$\widehat N_k=(-1)^k
\exp\left(\frac{\alpha k(k+1)}2\right)\widehat N_0.$$

If simple exponential expansion were the intended goal, a different model would use a constant per-step multiplier, $G_k=-e^{\alpha}G_{k-1}$, giving $G_k=(-1)^ke^{\alpha k}G_0$. This is an additional modeling choice, not the article's original Noroboro equation.

## 6. Iteration is different from taking a limit

Step 1 of the original article writes $O(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f^n(O_0)$ while describing perpetual cycling. An orbit is the indexed family $f^k(O_0)$. Its ordinary limit, if it exists, is a single limiting value. With fixed $f$ and $O_0$, the displayed limit has no remaining $t$ dependence.

For $f(z)=-z$ and $O_0\neq0$, the even subsequence equals $O_0$ and the odd subsequence equals $-O_0$. Their distinct limits show that the full sequence has no ordinary limit. Infinite iteration is coherent here; an ordinary convergent limit is not the right representation of the cycle. No replacement concept of limit is assumed.

## 7. Interpretation, novelty, and proof obligations

The following conclusions must be kept distinct:

- **Demonstrated:** the exact solutions, the original model's magnitude growth, and the modified model's two-step closure under the stated assumptions.
- **Equivalent to established mathematics:** the series is a polylogarithm specialization; its integer samples are the zeta function; the normalized discrete model is an ordinary sign-reversal two-cycle. No new theorem or priority claim is made for these facts.
- **Unresolved topology:** multiplying a complex scalar by $-1$ does not construct a Möbius strip. A Möbius interpretation needs a specified state space and identification, with a proof of non-orientability. In the complex plane itself, multiplication by $-1$ is a rotation and preserves orientation.
- **Unresolved fractality:** an infinite Fourier expansion alone does not establish a fractal set. One must define the geometric object and prove the intended scaling or dimension property in a specified parameter range.
- **Hypothesis/postulate:** applicability to physical emergence, cognition, or time requires observable definitions, units, a data-generating model, and predictions that can fail.
- **Unresolved metaphysical inference:** these recurrences presuppose a mathematical domain, rules, and initial data. They do not establish whether absolute nonexistence is possible. The mathematical zero state is not automatically identical to absolute nonexistence.

There are no experimental data, fitted parameters, or independent empirical replications in this repository. The numerical checks are independent checks of algebraic consequences, not independent evidence for a physical or metaphysical interpretation.

## 8. Reproducibility and a frozen acceptance test

For the cyclic objective, fix integer steps, real $s>1$, and nonzero initial data before evaluation. The criterion is exact two-step closure plus constant magnitude for every step. Proposition 2 proves that the normalized model meets that criterion; Proposition 1 shows that the original model fails it.

The next empirical study, if desired, must first define an observed state and a competing baseline. A suitable scientific claim cannot be selected after inspecting which model fits the observations.

`node scripts/verify.cjs` runs floating-point consistency checks, including direct iteration against closed-form predictions, boundary cases, the zero solution, and known zeta values. The browser uses an Euler–Maclaurin approximation for $\zeta(s)$; the displayed plot is illustrative and not a rigorous interval calculation. Its declared interactive domain is $1.25\leq s\leq6$, $0\leq\alpha\leq0.30$, and $0\leq k\leq20$.
